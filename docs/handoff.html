Read this before rebuilding it anywhere

The infrastructure works. The strategy has no demonstrated edge, and its centrepiece does not do what it claims: shuffling the regime model's own predictions into random order scores the same. Over ten years it returns +93% against SPY's +204%.

Moving the same rules to a different tool changes the plumbing, not the edge. If the goal is a nicer way to iterate, that is a fair reason to switch. If the goal is for it to start making money, the binding problem travels with you.

01 What it is

After each US close it classifies the market into a volatility regime, maps that regime to a target exposure, ranks fourteen large-cap names, sizes each position by risk rather than by dollar amount, and submits limit orders that fill at the next open. Every filled position immediately gets a stop order resting at the broker, so protection survives the process exiting.

It runs on GitHub Actions. Nothing needs to stay switched on.

02 The strategy, complete

This section is the migratable part. Everything else in the repository is plumbing.

Universe

SPY  QQQ  AAPL  MSFT  NVDA  META  AMZN
GOOGL  AMD  TSLA  AVGO  PLTR  COIN  SMCI

Daily bars. Split and dividend adjusted for indicators, raw prices for orders and stops. Getting that backwards makes a 4-for-1 split look like a 75% crash.

Step 1 — Classify the regime

Fit a Gaussian Hidden Markov Model on six volatility features of SPY. State count chosen by BIC from 3 to 7 candidates. A regime change must persist three consecutive bars before it is acted on. Confidence below 0.55 halves position size.

States are then re-sorted by measured volatility, not by return. This is why a state labelled "bull" can land in the high-volatility tier and be allocated defensively. It looks wrong on a dashboard until you know why.

Step 2 — Regime to target exposure

TierConditionTargetStop rule
Low vol—95%max(price − 3·ATR, EMA50 − 0.5·ATR)

EMA50 is the 50-period exponential moving average, ATR the 14-period average true range. Only rebalance when the gap to target exceeds 10%. Stop floors: at least 0.5·ATR below entry and at least 0.5% of price.

Step 4 — Size the position

shares = (equity × risk_pct) / |entry − stop|

Risk-based, not notional-based. A wide stop buys fewer shares, a tight stop more, so every trade loses roughly the same amount when it is wrong. Then capped, in order:

Step 5 — Exits

  • Stop loss — resting at the broker, placed the moment a fill lands. Tighten-only; a stop may never move away from price.
  • Take profit — entry + 2.0 × (entry − stop).
  • Trailing stop — Alpaca native, trail percent derived from ATR. Must be standalone; Alpaca forbids it as a bracket leg.

Step 6 — Circuit breakers

Levels set by how often they fire against SPY 2015–2026, not by what they return.

RuleLevelFires
Daily reduce5%0.6×/yr
Daily halt8%0.1×/yr
Weekly reduce10%0.2×/yr
Weekly halt15%never in 10.7 yrs
Peak halt25%1 yr in 11

These were previously tutorial day-trading numbers — a 3% daily halt fires about three times a year, and the 10% peak halt caught six years in eleven. A breaker that frequent is not protecting the strategy, it is replacing it. The system held 6% instead of 32% and took 565 trades where it should have taken 2,859.

Execution and schedule

Decide after the close, submit limit orders, fill at the next open. Limit offset 0.1% through the touch against a live quote, 0.5% when pricing off a stale bar close. Reject if the quote spread exceeds 1%, if the limit deviates more than 5% from reference, or if the stop would sit at or above the limit.

04 What the evidence says

Real Alpaca bars, 2016-01 to 2026-09, fourteen symbols, walk-forward, out of sample, with the real position limits applied.

It does not beat the index

ReturnWorst drawdownSharpe
This strategy+93%−21%0.93
SPY buy and hold+204%−34%0.83

Better risk-adjusted return and a third less drawdown, but less than half the money, because it holds about 30% and the cash earns nothing.

The regime model is not doing anything

Its own predictions were shuffled into random order — same mix of regimes, timing destroyed. If the model reads the market it has to beat that.

ArmSharpe
HMM regime0.93
No HMM at all0.89
Shuffled labels0.94

A gap of −0.014 against a shuffle spread of 0.025. The centrepiece adds no timing value. Whatever the system earns comes from the entry screen, the stop placement and the position sizing.

Three traps that produced false results here

  • Synthetic data. The loader silently returned a random walk when it could not reach the broker. Every backtest before 2026-09-10 was measured on noise. Verify any tool is loading real bars.
  • Backtest that was not the live system. A single-asset test with no position caps averaged 80% invested; the live system with caps averages 6–30%. Different strategies. Any backtest must model the per-position cap and the count limit.
  • No multiple-testing correction. Best-of-twenty over pure noise passes an uncorrected Sharpe nearly always. The best setting found here scores 0.789 on a deflated Sharpe against a 0.95 bar — not distinguishable from a lucky search.

The constraint no tool removes

Telling a good strategy from a lucky one, from live profit and loss alone, needs roughly seven years at this Sharpe. Live results cannot be the feedback loop. Any product promising a bot that learns from its own recent performance is selling noise-fitting.

06 Prompt to paste in

I want to build a daily US equity swing strategy on a paper account.

Universe: SPY QQQ AAPL MSFT NVDA META AMZN GOOGL AMD TSLA AVGO PLTR
COIN SMCI. Daily bars.

Entry: long only. A symbol is a candidate when its price is above its
own 50-period EMA. Rank candidates by 0.35 x (price above EMA50) +
0.30 x stop tightness in ATR terms + 0.15 x 20-day return, and take the
top names up to 12 concurrent positions.

Stop loss: max(price - 3 x ATR(14), EMA50 - 0.5 x ATR(14)), never less
than 0.5 x ATR or 0.5% of price below entry. Stops may only tighten.

Take profit: entry + 2 x (entry - stop).

Position sizing: shares = (equity x 0.00667) / (entry - stop), capped at
3% of equity per position and 80% gross exposure. Reject a new position
if its 60-day correlation to an existing one exceeds 0.85.

Circuit breakers: halve size after a 5% daily or 10% weekly drawdown.
Stop trading after 8% daily, 15% weekly, or 25% below the equity high.

Orders: limit orders submitted after the close, priced 0.1% through the
touch, filling at the next open.

Backtest this walk-forward on 10 years, out of sample, with per-position
caps applied, against three benchmarks: SPY buy-and-hold, a 200-day SMA
trend system, and random entry with the same exposure. Report max
drawdown and Sharpe, not just total return.

Tell me how many configurations you tested.

The last line matters more than the rest. If the tool cannot say how many configurations it tried, its best result cannot be interpreted — and the best result is the only one it will show you.
